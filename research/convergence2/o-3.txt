## Build "Revenant" — a town of AI ghosts that refuses to die

**One-liner:** A persistent, multiplayer haunted town where every ghost is its own durable actor — a long-running AI agent with memory, grudges and goals — and the ghosts gossip, scheme and spread hauntings *directly to each other* over Agent Relay. Players drop in from an iPhone, whisper a secret to one ghost, and watch it ripple through the whole town in real time. Kill the server mid-demo and the souls come right back, remembering everything. They're revenants. They don't die.

The theme *is* the architecture: **durability = the ghost that won't stay dead.** That single metaphor is why this wins both prizes at once.

### Why it maps perfectly onto both sponsors
- **Durable Actors (Terse)** — one actor per ghost. Each holds its own memory log, mood, relationships, and a long-running tick loop that calls OpenAI to decide what to do next. Persistent state that survives a process kill is literally the pitch. This is the clearest possible demo of what Durable Actors are *for*.
- **Agent Relay** — ghosts don't route through a central orchestrator copy-pasting messages. A ghost *whispers* to its neighbors; rumors, plans and hauntings propagate agent-to-agent. Emergent town drama with no conductor is the purest Agent Relay demo there is.

You use both, heavily and naturally — not bolted on. That's the "bonus points" the judges called out.

### The three demo money-shots (what actually wins the room)
1. **Propagation:** Two phones. Player A tells one ghost a secret → the room watches it spread ghost-to-ghost across a live relay graph. Visible, instant, magical.
2. **Durability:** `kill -9` the server on stage, restart it, the town resumes mid-conversation with every memory intact. "They're revenants." This is the moment that locks Best Durable Actors.
3. **Long-running / emergent:** Start the town at 11am and *leave it running all day*. By the 4pm demo the ghosts have an entire self-generated history — feuds, alliances, a haunting that got out of hand. You're not demoing a toy; you're demoing a world that lived while you weren't watching.
