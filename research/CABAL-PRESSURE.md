# CABAL PRESSURE: making four AIs read as conniving people in 2 s lines

Inputs: lanes/CABAL.md, contract/protocol.ts (rooms: attic, nursery, library, ballroom, chapel, crypt;
personas in HUNTERS). Lines below are subtitle text only (name tag is separate), each <= 70 chars.
Whispers use the contract lure templates verbatim.

## 1. Six principles

1. Dramatic irony. The audience sees the real counts; the hunter does not trust them. Laughter comes from
   watching someone confidently act on a lie we saw told. Why: the viewer feels smarter than the AI, which is
   the whole joke and proves the AI is "real" enough to be fooled.
   Example: (board: crypt 0) Medium: "kyle says the crypt. kyle has an honest little face."
2. Status games. Every line raises the speaker or lowers someone else. Exorcist is high status by not caring;
   Priest is starving for status; Skeptic gains it by tallying others; Medium gains it by being "chosen".
   Why: status transactions are what makes improv dialogue read as people, not exposition.
   Example: Medium: "priya came to me personally." Exorcist: "she picked you. out of four."
3. Confident wrongness. Certainty plus a wrong conclusion, delivered flat. Never hedge, never "maybe".
   Why: a hedge reads as a model; total certainty in nonsense reads as a person you know.
   Example: Skeptic: "board says the attic is empty. so they're in the attic."
4. Say/do gap (betrayal). A promise out loud, then the token lands elsewhere, then a one-line excuse.
   Why: Among Us vote reveal; betrayal is the cheapest proof of a private agenda (private Relay memory).
   Example: Skeptic: "I agreed out loud. I voted in private."
5. Callbacks with numbers. Reference beat N, a name, a tally. Then repeat the mistake.
   Why: memory across beats is visible proof of agency; the repeat is the punchline (rule of three).
   Example: Medium: "kyle lied to me in beat one. I will not be fooled again." (then is)
6. Specificity and the deflating straight line. Real name, real room, real count, then someone flattens it
   in five words. Why: generic spooky lines are slop; a specific line plus a deadpan reply is a joke shape
   (setup, flat puncture). The Exorcist is the default straight man.
   Example: Medium: "that's a consensus." Skeptic: "that's three people who want you there."

Line law for the generator: one idea, one concrete fact (name, room, count or beat), <= 70 chars,
lowercase, no exclamation marks, no "ghost" from the Skeptic ("subjects"), and the reply must lower or
puncture the line before it. Reject and fall back to section 2 if a line contains: boo, spooky, spirit,
presence, realm, beyond, veil, darkness, "let's", "dive", "!", or no proper noun and no number.

## 3. What shocks a judge, ranked

### First 15 s of the video (cold open, no title card, no logo, no QR, no tutorial)
- 0-2 s: the table, the carved map, crypt shows 0. Big italic card: "kyle whispers: Psst. Everyone's in
  The Crypt." The viewer instantly knows it is a lie.
- 2-6 s: Medium's needle swings to the crypt with "swayed by kyle". "kyle says the crypt. kyle has an honest
  little face."
- 6-9 s: Skeptic: "kyle is a dead stranger who typed one sentence."
- 9-12 s: vote reveal, one token per beat; the Priest promised the ballroom and slams the crypt.
- 12-15 s: cut to the house, flashlight on an empty crypt; cut to kyle's phone "The Medium believes you.
  +200"; cut to the real person grinning. Only then the title.

### Ranked moments to engineer (video and 2-min live)
1. A named human's lie lands. Board says 0, whisper says crowd, needle swings, hunter repeats it aloud with
   the name, sweep is empty, the liar's phone pops +200. One causal chain, visible in 6 s. Must happen in
   beat 1 of every demo; plant a human liar for the capture.
2. A judge becomes the liar (live). Hand a judge a phone before beat 1. Their own name in the Medium's mouth
   ("dan says the library. dan has kind eyes.") is the strongest moment available; nobody forgets it.
3. The vote betrayal. A promise out loud, a token slammed elsewhere, a one-line excuse. Needs the stage to
   show the promise and the vote back to back, one token per beat, not all at once.
4. The callback fooled again. Beat 3+: "kyle lied to me in beat one." then believes kyle. Proves private
   memory over Relay better than any architecture slide.
5. Possession finale. A room fills, goes red, an Exorcist line ("they've put their names on the door"),
   ghosts win, the house goes red. The ending shot.
6. The fourth-wall line from real data ("there's a guy in the front row. he's laughing.") only if driven by
   something real (players online, a judge's name); otherwise skip.
7. Resilience beat (kill -9 a hunter, it rejoins with its memory and holds the grudge). Keep to 5 s, show
   the grudge line, not the terminal.

### Cut
- Any beat where nobody lied or all four agree: no conflict, no joke. Skip it in the edit.
- Lobby, QR waiting, onboarding cards, picking UI longer than 2 s, score tables mid-round.
- The small chat ticker and more than one subtitle on screen at once.
- Architecture talk beyond one overlay line ("4 agents, private memory, Agent Relay").
- Spooky stings, generic horror music under dialogue, bot-looking names (bot_07) on screen; give bots
  human lowercase names for the capture.
- Lines with no name and no number; anything a hunter says that the audience cannot check on the board.
